One of the biggest communication challenges during my PhD was explaining hierarchical Gaussian Process (GP) regression, the statistical framework underlying GPMelt [1], a method I developed to analyse Thermal Proteome Profiling (TPP) datasets.

While the method was designed for biologists, its correct use requires understanding several highly abstract statistical concepts that are unfamiliar to most users.

To address this challenge, I created a series of educational resources combining videos, visual explanations, theory, and hands-on tutorials tailored to audiences ranging from experimental biologists to statistically trained users.

Challenges

Hierarchical Gaussian Process regression is a highly abstract statistical framework that can be intimidating even for quantitatively trained scientists. Yet, using GPMelt effectively requires understanding several interconnected concepts spanning multiple levels of abstraction, from the intuition behind Gaussian Process regression and its application to Thermal Proteome Profiling data, to the way biological experimental designs are represented within a hierarchical statistical model.

The communication challenge was therefore twofold: lowering the barrier created by mathematical complexity while preserving the concepts necessary for meaningful use of the method. At the same time, the resources had to work for audiences ranging from experimental biologists with limited statistical training to bioinformaticians and statisticians with very different levels of familiarity with Gaussian Processes.

The key challenge was to communicate the same underlying ideas across multiple levels of abstraction and audiences without sacrificing clarity, rigor, or engagement.

Approach

Rather than introducing the framework through mathematical formalism, I designed a series of short, visual-first videos that introduced the key concepts progressively, from intuitive explanations aimed at a broad audience to more advanced material for statistically trained users. The goal was not only to make the principles easier to understand, but to provide multiple entry points into the same underlying concepts.

Key design choices included:

  • prioritising conceptual understanding before introducing formal definitions;
  • adapting vocabulary, examples, and level of detail to each audience;
  • using visual metaphors, colour coding, and animation to make abstract concepts more tangible;
  • progressively increasing the level of abstraction, from intuitive explanations to more formal statistical reasoning.

Strengths

  • Adapting explanations to audiences with different levels of expertise;
  • Making complex concepts accessible without oversimplification;
  • Communicating across multiple levels of abstraction while preserving rigor;
  • Translation between disciplines and domains (e.g. from biological data to mathematical modelling; from statistical analysis to biological interpretation);
  • Connecting experimental, statistical, and computational perspectives;
  • Designing learning pathways that support progressive understanding, from intuition to formalization;
  • Creating high-quality visual explanations of abstract concepts (figures, animation, storytelling);
  • Developing concise, engaging educational content for self-directed learning.

Impact

These resources were designed to help users understand the reasoning behind the model, not only to learn how to use GPMelt in practice. By making the underlying statistical framework more approachable across different levels of expertise, they aimed to reduce the gap between method developers and end users.

This allows users to engage with the modelling framework at a depth appropriate to their needs and expertise, while maintaining a coherent conceptual link between the biological experiment and its statistical analysis. As a result, researchers can engage more confidently with the methodology, better understand its assumptions and limitations, and use the resulting analyses more critically and effectively.

Ultimately, the objective was to transform the model from a black box into a tool that users could meaningfully understand and build upon. By focusing on the underlying modelling principles rather than software usage alone, these resources provide a foundation for adapting and extending the framework to new experimental settings and data types.
