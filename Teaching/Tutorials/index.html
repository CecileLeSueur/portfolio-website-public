This tutorial complements the theoretical resources presented in Example 1 by focusing on the practical use of GPMelt [1]. While the videos explain the modelling framework and its underlying assumptions, this resource is designed to help users apply the methodology in practice and make informed decisions throughout the analysis process.

GPMelt combines probabilistic modelling, statistical analysis, and reproducible workflow execution into a flexible framework for analysing Thermal Proteome Profiling (TPP) datasets. This flexibility allows the framework to accommodate a wide range of experimental designs and analytical questions, but also requires users to understand how methodological choices influence the results.

The goal of the tutorial was therefore not simply to teach users how to run the workflow, but to help them understand the reasoning behind parameter choices, interpret outputs critically, and make effective use of the framework’s flexibility.

Challenges

GPMelt was developed as a flexible analytical framework designed to accommodate both existing and future Thermal Proteome Profiling (TPP) experimental designs. While this flexibility increases the applicability of the method, it also introduces additional complexity for users.

Many aspects of the analysis can be configured or adapted depending on the experimental design, the biological question, and the characteristics of the data. Making informed choices therefore requires more than simply following instructions or applying default settings: users need to understand how different parameters, assumptions, and visualisations influence the resulting analyses and interpretations.

Overall, the challenge was to design a tutorial that promotes understanding rather than execution and encourages users to move beyond default settings and black-box usage.

Approach

I designed the tutorial as a guided learning experience that combines practical usage with conceptual understanding, rather than presenting the workflow as a sequence of commands to execute.

Key design choices included:

  • structuring the tutorial around the complete analytical workflow, from data preparation and parameter selection to result interpretation;
  • combining practical instructions with explanations of the modelling assumptions and analytical decisions underlying the workflow;
  • explaining the role and impact of key parameters, including the situations in which different settings may be appropriate, and illustrating it through examples drawn from multiple experimental datasets;
  • providing copy-paste ready code examples to minimise technical friction and allow users to focus on the concepts being introduced;
  • integrating high-quality visualisations alongside code to generate them and explaination on how to interpret them.

Strengths

  • Designing user-centred learning experiences for complex technical tools;
  • Bridging theory and practice* to support informed decision-making**;
  • Encouraging autonomy and critical thinking rather than procedural execution;
  • Communicating modelling assumptions, parameter choices, trade-offs, and methodological consequences clearly;
  • Reducing technical barriers to adoption thanks to code examples without sacrificing conceptual understanding;
  • Creating interactive educational resources that combine code, visualisation, and explanation.

Impact

GPMelt was developed with the ambition of becoming an actionable statistical framework rather than a black-box analysis tool. Its flexibility was intentionally designed to accommodate not only existing experimental designs, but also the increasingly diverse methodologies emerging within the Thermal Proteome Profiling field and beyond.

By helping users understand the reasoning behind the framework and the consequences of different analytical choices, the tutorial enables them to understand how those choices influence the outputs, interpretations, and conclusions that can be drawn from the data. This gives researchers the confidence to adapt the methodology to their own scientific questions rather than simply applying predefined workflows.

Ultimately, the objective was to provide a foundation that supports both rigorous analysis and methodological creativity, enabling new experimental designs to be driven by scientific needs rather than constrained by available analytical tools.
